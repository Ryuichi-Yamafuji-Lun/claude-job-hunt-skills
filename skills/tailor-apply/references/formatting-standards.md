# Resume formatting standards (sourced)

These ground the rules in `SKILL.md`. Prefer university career centers and recruiter guidance over SEO content farms.

## Bullet count: 3–4 per role (hard cap)
- UW Career Center: 2–5 bullets per experience, the only sourced count found. 3–4 is the working cap.
- Overruns creep in when new content gets appended instead of replacing a bullet. Count before sending.

## Typical university career-center guidance (e.g. USC Career Center)
- 1 page if under ~10 years of experience
- Times New Roman / Arial, 11–12pt, ~1" margins
- ATS-safe: no columns, graphics, photos, or decorative fonts; PDF or Word
- Reverse chronological · no pronouns · action verbs · accomplishments over duties · quantify · drop irrelevant items

## Harvard Office of Career Services (settles bullet length)
- "Each line should be a phrase rather than a full sentence." This is the basis for one-line bullets.
- Written "for people who scan quickly": specific, active, fact-based; "express, not impress"
- Top mistakes: spelling, missing contact info, passive voice, not concise/skimmable, no results, too long
- Start lines with an action verb, never a date; quantify; balance white space

## University of Washington Career Center
- careers.uw.edu/resources/resume-writing-guide/
- "A marketing document... not an autobiography"; ATS-friendly

## Google recruiters
- XYZ formula: "Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]"
- Quantify everything · city + state only · no objective statement · no "references available" · active verbs

## Common drafting slips to catch in review
- Pronouns sneaking into high-agency bullets ("a codebase I had not written" → "in an unfamiliar codebase")
- A length pass that removes the Z (the "by doing" part) from an XYZ bullet
- A bullet ending on a dangling fragment after trimming
